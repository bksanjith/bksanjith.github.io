Solar food dryer

A low-cost drying box with an exhaust fan that only runs when the air inside is humid enough to be worth moving.

The brief

Design Thinking Project III ties together Designing Energy Systems and Modelling Uncertainty. The task was a solar-powered food drying device, tested outdoors between 9am and 11am and judged on grams of water removed from a wet sponge.

  • $100 budget for the group
  • No larger than 75 × 75 cm
  • Target: at least 4.0 g of water removed in 30 minutes

The build

The enclosure is a styrofoam box used as a frame, with acrylic windows on the two long sides and the top to let light in and keep heat trapped. The internal chamber is 36.5 × 19.0 × 17.0 cm.

I did the prototyping and electronics. Instead of running the fan nonstop, an ESP32-S3 reads humidity inside the box and switches the exhaust fan on at 50% and off at 35% relative humidity. The gap between the two stops the fan flickering on and off. The firmware also logs every reading to CSV so we could analyse each run afterwards.

We ruled out mirrors to concentrate sunlight because of the risk of scorching the sample.

Results

Original design4.2 g in 30 min
Modified design7.1 g in 30 min, 13.4 g in 60 min
Five repeat trials, 40 min eachMean 8.52 g, 95% CI 7.87 to 9.17 g
SpecificationAt least 4.0 g in 30 min
Solar availability94.8%, Wilson lower bound 92.5% against a 90% target

What I took away

Repeating trials and putting a confidence interval on the result changed how we talked about the design. "It works" became "it works this well, this reliably", which is a far more useful thing to tell someone deciding whether to use it.
