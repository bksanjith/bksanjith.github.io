Solar drip watering retrofit

Converting an off-the-shelf drip watering unit from a battery that needed regular charging to a self-powered solar device that hangs from a tree branch.

/ The Problem

I bought an automatic drip watering system online. It ran on a 3.7 V battery that lasted up to two months between charges, which meant someone had to remember to take it down and charge it. I wanted it to look after itself.

/ The Build
  • Power: 6 V 1 W solar panel feeding a PWM charge controller and a rechargeable lithium cell
  • Load: the pump draws about 1.27 A at 3.7 V, for 60 seconds, five times a day
  • Enclosure: designed in Fusion 360 and printed in PLA, with the panel as an overhanging canopy so rain runs off and the display and buttons stay usable
  • Mounting: designed to hang from a branch, with the pipe exits on the underside

/ What Went Wrong

My first battery choice, a small 400 mAh cell, cut out as soon as the pump started. The pump pulls about 1.27 A, roughly three times the cell's rated capacity (around 3C), and the cell simply couldn't supply current that fast. The motor tested fine on its own, so the fault was the power source, not the pump.

Lesson: size the battery for peak current, not just for total energy. A cell can hold enough charge for the day and still be unable to deliver it fast enough.
