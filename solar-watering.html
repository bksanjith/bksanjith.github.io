The problem

I bought an automatic drip watering system online. It ran on a 3.7 V battery that lasted up to two months between charges, so someone had to remember to take it down and charge it. I wanted it to look after itself.

The build

  • Power: 6 V 1 W solar panel feeding a PWM charge controller and a rechargeable lithium cell
  • Load: the pump draws about 1.27 A at 3.7 V, for 60 seconds, five times a day
  • Enclosure: designed in Fusion 360 and printed in PLA, with the panel as an overhanging canopy so rain runs off and the display and buttons stay usable
  • Mounting: hangs from a branch, with the pipe exits on the underside
Image to add: enclosure in Fusion 360

What went wrong

My first battery, a small 400 mAh cell, cut out as soon as the pump started. The pump pulls about 1.27 A, roughly three times the cell's rated capacity, and the cell couldn't supply current that fast. The motor tested fine on its own, so the fault was the power source.

Lesson: size a battery for peak current, not just total energy. A cell can hold enough charge for the day and still be unable to deliver it fast enough.
